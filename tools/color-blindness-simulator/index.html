Protanopia

P

Red-blind: missing or anomalous L-cones (long wavelength). Reds appear darker, often confused with greens and browns.

Prevalence: ~1% of males, ~0.01% of females

Deuteranopia

D

Green-blind: missing or anomalous M-cones (medium wavelength). Greens appear muted, confused with reds and browns. Most common form.

Prevalence: ~1.5% of males, ~0.01% of females

Tritanopia

T

Blue-blind: missing or anomalous S-cones (short wavelength). Blues appear greenish, yellows appear pinkish. Very rare.

Prevalence: ~0.001% of population (males + females)

Achromatopsia

A

Total color blindness (monochromacy): no cone function. Only shades of gray are perceived. Extremely rare.

Prevalence: ~0.003% of population

Designing for Accessibility

Color should never be the only way to convey information. Supplement color with icons, patterns, text labels, or underlines. Use sufficient contrast ratios (4.5:1 for normal text per WCAG AA). Check your palettes with our Color Contrast Checker to ensure readability for all users.
